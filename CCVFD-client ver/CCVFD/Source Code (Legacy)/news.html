Community Wildfire Mitigation • Posted October 6, 2026

Wildfire Mitigation Outreach Comes to Clear Creek October 18

Clear Creek landowners are invited to an educational presentation on forest health, hazardous-fuels reduction, and wildfire resilience on Sunday, October 18, from 2:00 to 4:00 PM at the Clear Creek Lodge & Restaurant, 166 Clear Creek Road.

The presentation is being organized through the National Forest Foundation and the Southwest Idaho All-Lands Partnership, working with local, state, and federal partners. The partnership is focused on reducing wildfire risk across public and private lands in Southwest Idaho, including ongoing work in and around Clear Creek.

Current private-land project outreach is intended primarily for larger landowners with approximately 10 acres or more. Neighboring landowners may also be able to participate together when their combined project area meets the program's scale. The work includes a landowner match, and the outreach meeting is a good opportunity to learn what that means, ask questions, and determine whether a property or group of properties may fit the program.

A companion outreach event will be held Saturday, October 17, from 2:00 to 4:00 PM at the Wilderness Ranch Fire House, 7 Wilderness Way. Clear Creek residents are especially encouraged to attend the Sunday presentation at the Lodge, but the events are educational and community members interested in wildfire mitigation are welcome to learn more.

Burning & Fire Safety • Posted October 6, 2026

Burn Season Reminder: What Changes After October 20?

With fall weather arriving, many residents are preparing for slash-pile and debris burning. The key date to remember is October 20.

Boise County's current burning ordinance defines the county closed season as July 1 through October 20, unless the Board of County Commissioners adjusts those dates. Idaho Department of Lands also requires free burn permits for most burning outside city limits from May 10 through October 20.

State and federal Stage 1 fire restrictions in the Boise Fire Restriction Area were lifted effective September 3, 2026, but that did not cancel Boise County's separate seasonal burning restrictions. The county closed season remains the controlling local rule through October 20.

October 20 is not an automatic “all clear.”

Before any burn, check current weather and wind, Idaho DEQ air-quality rules, land-manager requirements, local fire restrictions, and any emergency orders. Keep water and tools on site, never leave a burn unattended, and call 9-1-1 immediately if fire escapes or conditions become unsafe.

Archived Update • September 3, 2026

Stage 1 Restrictions Lifted in the Boise Fire Restriction Area

State and federal land-management agencies lifted Stage 1 fire restrictions in the Boise Fire Restriction Area at 12:01 AM on September 3 after wildfire danger decreased. The agencies emphasized that fire season was not over and urged continued caution. Boise County's separate seasonal burning ban remained in effect.

Archived Incident • Summer 2026

Claremont Fire: Recovery and Trail Repair

The Claremont Fire began July 6 in the Boise Foothills and became one of the region's major early-summer wildfire incidents. By August 2, the Bureau of Land Management had reopened all affected trails except a segment of the 8th Street Motorcycle Trail, while repair and rehabilitation work continued. Ridge to Rivers reported in September that reconstruction and resource-protection work was still underway in burned portions of the Foothills.

CCVFD is keeping this item as an archive because the incident offered important lessons for evacuation readiness, mutual-aid capacity, air quality, travel planning, and post-fire landscape recovery.

Archived Incident • Summer 2026

Mutual Aid and Community Action During Clear Creek Structure Fire

During a major structure fire in the Clear Creek area, CCVFD worked alongside Wilderness Ranch Fire Protection District, Thorn Creek Volunteer Fire Department, Robie Creek Volunteer Fire Department, Boise County Sheriff's Office, Idaho Department of Lands, SIRPA, U.S. Forest Service resources, emergency medical responders, and local residents. The incident underscored how important mutual aid, water supply, clear access, and prepared neighbors are in a rural response area.

CCVFD remains grateful to every responding agency and neighbor who helped during the incident.
